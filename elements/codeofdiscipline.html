CODE OF DISCIPLINE

It is only when one knows the rules of the game can he participate in it happily and effectively. For the information and guidance of all concerned, the herewith-attached Code of Discipline is hereby polished, with the injunction that strict compliance thereto be made in our day-to-day operations for the sole purpose of eliminating misunderstanding. Further, you are assured that Uniform application and unbiased implementation of this Code will be strictly observed. It is the first duty of ALL employees to know these Rules and Regulations. It is the policy of the Company to undertake disciplinary actions with due process against employees violating the Company Policies and Procedures.

Section I – Implementing Guidelines

The Immediate Supervisor/Superior will be responsible for maintaining discipline and implementing the rules and regulations of the Company.

The Human Resources Department will be consulted first before any implementation of any disciplinary action and make recommendations based on the Company Policies and Procedures to ensure accuracy and consistency in interpretation and administration.

The employees are responsible for understanding the policies and procedures. Should there be any clarification, they may consult and confer with their Immediate Supervisor or the HR – Supervisor. Please be reminded that “Ignorance does not excuse employees from disciplinary sanctions”.

All employees have the right to fair investigation and due process before any disciplinary action is executed. The due process provides every employee the opportunities to explain and state in writing the reasons for his/her conduct and or behavior in order that Management will come up with a fair, equitable and objective decision.

Management may add, delete or revise any portion of the Code of Discipline as deemed necessary. Such changes will be posted and disseminated to all employees before implementation.

Management has the prerogative to impose a higher or lower penalty other than what is specified for a particular offense if the damage caused and the surrounding circumstances so warrant.

Dismissal takes effect within a twelve-month period from the date when the first offense of the same nature was committed.

In cases where there is a risk in the lives of others and to Company property, the employee under investigation shall be placed on preventive suspension with a maximum of thirty (30) days without pay.

Section II – Offenses and their Corresponding Penalties

Any acts of omissions or commission leading to failure in obeying rules and regulations of the Company Policy in relation to any offense committed. These are grouped under the three (3) classifications:

Minor offense – refers to offenses that are of lesser degree. It can include misdemeanor cases and infraction cases, which are of less serious nature.
Serious offense – refers to offenses that are sober in nature but would not automatically constitute a threatening outcome to the company.
Grave offense – refers to offenses of a very serious or critical in nature or being about totally threatening a seriously bad outcome to the company.

Minor Offense
1st Offense – Written Reprimand
2nd Offense – Written Reprimand
3rd Offense – 5 Days Suspension
4th Offense – 10 Days Suspension
5th Offense – 15 Days Suspension
6th Offense – Dismissal

Serious Offense
1st Offense – Written Reprimand with 5 Days Suspension
2nd Offense – 10 Days Suspension
3rd Offense – 15 Days Suspension
4th Offense – Dismissal

Grave Offense
1st Offense – Dismissal

Section III – Types of Disciplinary Actions

Written Reprimand
An action wherein an erring employee is informed through a written memorandum regarding his/her unacceptable conduct which considered enough to merit as corrective action.

Suspension
An action placing an erring employee under suspension, if his continued presence at work poses a serious threat to the life or property of his/her employer or his/her co-employees; or may compromise the result of an investigation involving him/her.

Dismissal
It is the complete separation of an employee (for cause) from the Company. The Company reserves the right to institute court action against the erring employee/s when the interest of the Company so requires.

Section IV – Application of Penalties

The penalties for successive violations of the same rule (other than in those cases where a single violation of rule warrants dismissal) are progressively more severe than the penalty of the first violation.

Where a single act constitutes two or more offenses under this Code or when the offense is a necessary means of committing the other, the penalty for the more/most serious offense shall be imposed.

If at the time of commission of the latest offense, the employee has previously violated at least two (2) separate rules embraced in this Code or of other promulgated Company work rules, all were committed within a 12-month period, the latest offense shall be punishable by a higher degree of penalty or a penalty of a more serious type of offense shall be implemented.

Emergency Leave – refers to a personal leave because of the death, illness, injury or medical emergency of or an “urgent matter” concerning, a specified family member. This is unplanned or out of the employee’s control, and raises the possibility of serious negative consequences, including emotional harm, if not responded to.

Section VI – REPUBLIC ACT 7877 Sexual Harassment Policy and Procedures

WHEN IS SEXUAL HARASSMENT COMMITTED?

Any person who, having authority, influence or moral ascendancy over another in a work or training or education environment demands, requests, or otherwise requires any sexual favor from the other, regardless of whether the demand request or requirement for submission is accepted by the object of the said act in a work related or employment environment;

A sexual favor is made as a condition in the hiring employment or re-employment or continued employment of said individual, or in granting said individual favorable compensation, terms, conditions, promotions or privileges; or

The refusal to grant the sexual favor results in limiting, segregating or classifying the employee which in any way would discriminate, deprive or diminish employment opportunities or otherwise adversely affect said employee;

The above acts would impair the employee’s rights or privileges under existing labor laws;

The above acts would result in an intimidating, hostile or offensive environment for the employee.

FORMS OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT

1. Persistently telling smutty jokes to a co-employee who has indicated she/he finds them offensive.
2. Taunting a co-employee with constant talk of sex or sexual insinuations.
3. Displaying offensive pictures or publication in the workplace.
4. Asking a co-employee intimate questions regarding his/her sexual activities.
5. Making offensive hand or body gestures at a co-employee during and after working hours.
6. Making obscene phone calls and/or text messages to a co-employee during and after working hours.
7. Unnecessarily brushing up against a co-employee’s body.
